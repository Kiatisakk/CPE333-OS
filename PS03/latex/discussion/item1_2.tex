\texttt{nice} sets a process's \emph{niceness}, a scheduling hint from $-20$ to
$+19$ where higher means lower priority. It does not cap or reserve CPU time,
only weights the scheduler's choice when several processes are ready at once.
\texttt{nice -n 10 <command>} starts one at that niceness; \texttt{renice -n 5
-p <pid>} changes one already running. Raising niceness is unprivileged,
\emph{lowering} it is not --- hence the failure above: a user may make their own
work less important, but not more important than everyone else's, which needs
\texttt{CAP\_SYS\_NICE}.
