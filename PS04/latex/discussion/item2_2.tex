Removing \texttt{extern} turns \texttt{int x;} inside \texttt{main()} into a
local definition on the stack, shadowing the global variable. Because this local
variable is uninitialised, reading it invokes undefined behaviour --- as the
compiler warns: \texttt{`x' is used uninitialized} --- outputting arbitrary
stack content and a stack-range address. Meanwhile \texttt{display()} is
unchanged and continues to access the global variable in the data segment. The
two addresses printed within a single run lie in entirely different regions of
memory, which is the clearest evidence that they are two distinct variables.
