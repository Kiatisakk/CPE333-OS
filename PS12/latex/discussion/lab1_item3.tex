% Discussion for lab1_item3 -- never overwritten by make_latex.py.

Deleting level~1 breaks a level-2 symbolic link, and deleting the source breaks
both symbolic links; by contrast, a level-2 hard link survives the deletion of
both the source and level~1.

Symbolic links resolve by following pathnames hop by hop
(\texttt{l2\_sym.txt -> l1\_sym.txt -> src\_s.txt}). Removing the intermediate
link \texttt{l1\_sym.txt} severs the chain so reading \texttt{l2\_sym.txt}
fails immediately, even though \texttt{src\_s.txt} still exists; restoring
\texttt{l1\_sym.txt} and removing \texttt{src\_s.txt} leaves both links
dangling.

Hard links do not form a chain of indirection: \texttt{ln l1\_hard.txt
l2\_hard.txt} (and \texttt{mklink /H l2\_hard.txt l1\_hard.txt} on Windows)
binds \texttt{l2\_hard.txt} directly to the same inode/MFT entry as
\texttt{src\_h.txt}, bringing the Linux link count to~3. Removing the source
reduces the count to~2 and removing level~1 reduces it to~1, leaving
\texttt{l2\_hard.txt} fully readable on both Linux and Windows.
